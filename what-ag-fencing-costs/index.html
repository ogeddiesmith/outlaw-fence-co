What Agricultural Fencing Costs

Why ag fence is quoted by the job and not by the foot

A per-foot price describes wire and line posts. That is the cheapest part of the fence and the part least likely to fail. It says nothing about the braces, and the braces are where the money is.

Two jobs at identical footage on identical wire can price very differently. One is a square 40 with four corners and one gate. The other follows a creek and a county road, changes direction a dozen times, needs three gates and crosses water. Same wire, same feet, different fence.

Residential fence gets quoted by the foot because a backyard is a rectangle with two gates. Acreage is not a rectangle.

What actually drives the number

Footage, and the shape of it

Total feet sets the material order and the days on site. The shape of those feet sets everything else. Ask yourself how many times the line turns before you compare bids, because that is the question the bid is really answering.

Brace count

Every end, every corner and every gate opening takes a braced assembly. Most direction changes take one too.

A brace is two or three posts, a horizontal rail, pins, brace wire and the labor to set it plumb and pull it tight. That is roughly [PRICE_TBD] each before a single foot of wire goes up. On a short run with a lot of corners, braces can be the majority of the bid.

This is the line item to look at when one bid comes in far under the others. Cheap fence is usually not cheaper wire. It is fewer braces, shorter braces, or an end post pounded in and hoped for.

Post spacing, and whether posts are driven or augered

Spacing follows the fence type. Barbed and woven wire run line posts on tight centers with stays between. High tensile runs them much wider, because the load is carried in the tensioned wire rather than in the posts. Wider spacing means fewer posts, but it is not automatically cheaper: high tensile costs more to brace correctly and more to tension correctly.

How the post goes in matters more to the price than most people expect.

  • Driven. Faster, and in ground that allows it, a better hold, because the soil around the post was never disturbed. Roughly [PRICE_TBD] per line post.
  • Augered and set. Slower, more material, more cost. It is what a gate post needs, and what shallow soil or rock forces. Roughly [PRICE_TBD] per post.

Rock

The biggest single swing in this market. Spokane County sits on basalt and it is not evenly distributed, so two neighbors can get very different fences for the same money.

Tell us on the first call if you or a neighbor have hit rock on that ground. Where the line has to be drilled, figure [PRICE_TBD] per hole. A bid that does not say what happens when it hits rock is a bid that will be having that conversation with you halfway through the job.

Clearing and brush

The line has to be clear before fence goes in it. Timber, brush, blackberry, stumps, old wire grown into trees. Clearing is billed by time, roughly [PRICE_TBD].

Taking out the old fence is separate again: pulling staples, rolling wire, pulling or digging posts, loading and hauling. Old barbed wire is heavy and heavy is what the dump charges for. Figure [PRICE_TBD] per foot for removal and disposal.

Wire type and gauge

Choice What it changes
Barbed wire, 4 strand or 5 Five for real cattle pressure, four where the line is a boundary more than a barrier. Fewer strands is less wire and fewer staples, not a different fence philosophy
Galvanizing class A heavier zinc coating costs more per roll and stays out of the scrap pile longer. On a fence you intend to keep for decades this is the cheapest upgrade on the list
Woven wire: knot type A hinge knot gives when an animal hits it and can be worked loose over time. A fixed knot holds its shape and holds tension, costs more, and is what uneven ground and hard-pressing stock call for
Woven wire: height, line wires and stay spacing A 47 inch fence with 6 inch stays and a 32 inch fence with 12 inch stays are different animals and different money. Match it to what you are containing, not to what is on sale
No-climb 2x4 mesh The horse answer. Costs more per foot than field fence and does not let a hoof through
High tensile More per foot in hardware (strainers, springs, insulators, an energizer if it is electric) and more skill in the bracing. Pays back on long runs and rolling ground

Gate count and width

Every gate needs a braced hinge post, which means every gate is really a gate plus an assembly. A 4 foot walk gate, a 12 foot tube gate and a 16 foot gate are three different prices. Automated entries add power, an operator and a trench.

Figure [PRICE_TBD] per gate hung and braced, by width.

Access and haul distance

Whether a loaded truck and trailer can reach the line, or whether every post and roll gets moved out there another way, is a real number of hours. So is the drive. Mobilization runs about [PRICE_TBD] per job and it does not scale with footage, which is why it hurts a small job and disappears into a big one. There is more on distance in service area.

The ground

Sidehill work is slower. Low spots need a tie-down or a deadman so stock cannot push under. Rises need an extra post. Water crossings need a floodgate arrangement built to let go, because the alternative is losing the fence and the crossing both in the first high water.

Corrals and handling equipment do not price by the foot at all. A pen system is designed around how cattle move through it, and the headgate, the tub or sweep, the alley width and the chute drive the number far more than the perimeter does.

What a bid should tell you before you sign it

Ask every outfit you are talking to for these, ours included. If a bid will not put them in writing, you are not comparing the same fence.

  • Wire spec by name, gauge and coating class, not just "field fence"
  • Post material, diameter and set depth, and whether it is driven or augered
  • Line post spacing in feet
  • Brace count and brace type, stated as a number
  • Gate count, width and material
  • Who clears the line, and who hauls off the old fence
  • What happens when the job hits rock, in dollars, in writing
  • Whether the price shown includes sales tax
  • The warranty, in writing, including what voids it
  • Who is responsible for the property line, and confirmation that utility locates get called in before anything goes in the ground

One thing about tax in Washington

A fence contractor in Washington charges retail sales tax on the whole contract price, labor included, not just on materials. If one bid looks lower than another, check whether tax is inside the number before you decide anything. That difference alone can be larger than the difference between the two fences.
