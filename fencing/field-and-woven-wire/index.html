Field and Woven Wire Fencing

Field fence is the workhorse on small acreage around Spokane and across the state line. If you have five to forty acres, mixed stock, or a perimeter that has to hold something smaller than a cow, this is usually the fence. Call [PHONE].

Woven wire costs more per foot than barbed wire and it holds a wider range of animals. It is also more demanding to build, for one reason: it is a continuous sheet, and the whole sheet loads the corner at once.

How to read a field fence number

Field fence is sold by a three-part designation. A roll marked 1047-6 means:

Digit group Meaning In 1047-6
First one or two digits Number of horizontal line wires 10 line wires
Next two digits Height of the fabric in inches 47 inches tall
Final digit Spacing between vertical stay wires, in inches 6 inch stays

Six inch stay spacing is the standard for horses and small stock. Twelve inch stay spacing costs less and is fine for cattle, which are not going to put a foot through a twelve inch gap. If a bid says "field fence" and nothing else, ask which one.

Hinge joint or fixed knot?

This is the decision that sets both the price and the service life. There is no third option worth discussing.

Hinge joint. The vertical stay wraps around the horizontal line wire and pivots on it. That hinge is the point of the design: hit the fence and it gives, then springs back. It is the cheaper fabric and it is genuinely appropriate on low-pressure ground.

Its two failure modes are both in the joint. The stay can slide along the line wire, so the squares distort and the graduated spacing is gone. And the joint work-hardens from flexing, so after enough cycles it breaks at the wrap.

Fixed knot. A separate piece of wire is wrapped around the intersection and locks the vertical to the horizontal. The stay cannot slide. The square keeps its shape under steady animal pressure instead of relaxing into a parallelogram.

Fixed knot fabric is normally built from high tensile wire, which means two more things follow: it can be tensioned harder, and it can run on wider post spacing without sagging. The higher fabric price is partly bought back in fewer posts.

Hinge joint Fixed knot
Joint behavior under pressure Gives, springs back, can slip Locked, holds the square
Wire Usually standard tensile Usually high tensile
Post spacing Closer Wider
Fabric cost Lower Higher
Service life under steady pressure Shorter Longer
Best fit Low-pressure perimeter, cattle on open pasture Horses, goats, small paddocks, anything leaned on daily

The short version: if animals will be standing against this fence, pay for fixed knot. If they will mostly ignore it, hinge joint is honest money.

Why the small squares go at the bottom

Look at a roll of field fence end on and the openings are tight at the bottom and progressively taller toward the top. That is graduated spacing, and it is deliberate.

  • Feet stay out. A hoof through a large opening at ground level is how animals get cut and how fences get wrecked. An animal that gets a leg in fights the fence, and it wins.
  • Small stock stays in. Lambs, kids, and calves go under and through, not over. The containment happens in the bottom twelve inches.
  • Dogs and coyotes stay out at the same level, for the same reason.
  • Nothing pushes at the top. So the wire spacing widens up there. You are not paying for steel where no load exists.

Install the roll upside down and you have built a fence that costs the same and does none of that. It happens.

Sheep and goat mesh

Goats are the hardest common animal to fence and they need their own fabric.

  • Uniform four inch by four inch squares. Not graduated. The reason is horns. A goat that puts its head through a taller opening cannot pull it back out, and a horned goat caught in a fence in summer is a dead goat. Four by four is small enough that the head does not go through in the first place.
  • Goats climb and lean. They will stand on a fence. That is steady vertical load in a spot barbed wire never sees, and it is a fixed knot argument, not a hinge joint one.
  • Sheep go under. The bottom of the fabric has to sit close to grade the whole way, which is a terrain problem, covered below.

Cattle-grade field fence used on goats is the single most common miss on hobby-farm property, and it usually gets replaced inside two seasons.

Wire gauge

Standard-grade field fence is commonly 12.5 gauge on the top and bottom line wires, with 14.5 gauge filler and stay wires. The heavier top and bottom carry the tension and take the abuse; the fillers hold the pattern.

Fixed knot high tensile fabric is commonly 12.5 gauge throughout, at a much higher strength rating, which is what allows it to be pulled tighter.

Galvanizing follows the same logic as barbed wire. Class 3 carries a heavier zinc coating than Class 1 and lasts considerably longer in wet ground. On a fence that is expensive to replace, the coating class matters more than the fabric brand.

Why woven wire needs more brace than barbed wire

A five strand barbed fence dead-ends five separate wires, each carrying its own tension, and each one can be pulled and adjusted independently.

A 47 inch sheet of woven wire dead-ends ten tensioned line wires into one assembly at the same instant, distributed up the full height of the post. That changes the engineering in two ways:

  1. Total load is higher. More wires, and on fixed knot fabric each wire is pulled harder.
  2. The load is spread up the post, not concentrated near the ground. The post is being pushed over as much as pulled. Depth of set matters more, not less.

So woven wire gets longer brace rails, larger diameter corner and end posts, deeper sets, and a double H on long pulls and soft ground. Every corner gets a full assembly. A single post driven at a corner will lean, and when it leans the entire sheet relaxes at once. With barbed wire you lose one strand. With woven wire you lose the fence.

This is covered in detail on braces and corners, and it is the part of the bid worth comparing between contractors.

Terrain: a sheet does not follow ground the way strands do

Individual strands drape independently. A 47 inch sheet cannot. It is rigid vertically, and on uneven ground it does one of two things:

  • In a dip or swale, it bridges. The fabric spans the low spot and leaves an open gap underneath, which is exactly where sheep, goats, calves, and dogs go through.
  • On a crest, it lifts. The bottom of the fabric comes off the ground and the fence rides up.

Three ways to handle it, and they get used together:

  1. Shorten the post spacing through the grade change and roll the fabric down to grade, pinning the bottom wire.
  2. Break the stretch at the grade change. End the pull at a braced assembly, start a fresh pull on the new grade. More assemblies, more cost, correct fence.
  3. Run a separate bottom wire below the fabric to close a shallow gap where a full break is not warranted.

Curves get the same treatment as barbed wire, only more so. Tension wants to pull a curve straight, and a sheet under tension pulls hard. Posts go on the outside of the arc, spacing tightens, and a tight radius gets broken into short straight runs between assemblies instead of forced.

Drainages and creek crossings need a plan. A sheet of woven wire across a seasonal drainage catches debris in spring runoff and takes the fence, the posts, and the corner with it. That crossing gets a break-away section or a floodgate, not fabric.

Stretching woven wire

The fabric is pulled with a stretcher bar that grips every line wire across the full height at once, so the whole sheet tensions together. Grabbing three or four wires with fence pliers deforms the sheet, leaves the rest slack, and locks that distortion in permanently.

On hinge joint fabric the tension curves are the gauge. Those are the crimps built into the horizontal line wires. Trade practice is to pull until roughly a quarter to a third of the crimp is drawn out, then stop. Pull the crimps flat and there is nothing left to absorb a cold snap or an impact, and the fence starts breaking wires.

Fixed knot high tensile fabric has no crimps. It is pulled to a measured tension instead.
