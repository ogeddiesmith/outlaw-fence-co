Have this ready before you call

You do not need every answer. But the more of this you have, the closer the number on the phone will be to the number in the bid.

What we need Why it changes the job
Approximate footage Pace it, drive it, or pull it off your parcel map. Rough is fine. It sets whether this is a half day or three weeks of work
How many corners, ends and direction changes Every one of them is a braced assembly, and braces are most of what you are paying for
What you are keeping in or out Cattle, horses, sheep, goats, hogs, dogs, deer. The animal picks the wire, the height and the spacing. A fence that holds cattle will not hold goats
Terrain Flat, sidehill, timber, draws, creek crossing, standing water. Also whether you or a neighbor have hit rock on that ground
Condition of the existing fence Bare ground, old wire that has to come out, or a line worth rebuilding on. Pulling staples, rolling wire, pulling posts and hauling it off is its own line item and old barbed wire is heavy
Access for equipment Can a truck and trailer reach the line. Is there an opening wide enough. Are there wet spots that will not carry a loaded trailer until later in the year
Gates: how many and how wide And what has to fit through. A pickup, a stock trailer and a swather are three different gates, and each one needs a braced hinge post
Whether the property line is surveyed Pins and a survey, or "the old fence has always been there." Building on the wrong line is the most expensive mistake in this trade and the cheapest one to avoid
For electric: power and ground Where an energizer could live, and whether the soil holds enough moisture for the ground field to work
When you need it standing Turnout date, weaning, a closing date, a lease. Say the real deadline
